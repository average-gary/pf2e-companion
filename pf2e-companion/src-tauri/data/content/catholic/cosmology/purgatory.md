# Purgatory — the Church Suffering

The Catholic-distinctive intermediate state. Per Catechism §1030–1032,
purgatory is the **final purification of the elect** — those who die
in God's grace and friendship but imperfectly purified, who undergo
purification after death "so as to achieve the holiness necessary to
enter the joy of heaven." Anchored doctrinally on 2 Maccabees 12:46
(Judas Maccabeus offers sacrifice for the dead "that they might be
loosed from sins") and 1 Corinthians 3:15 ("he himself will be saved,
but only as through fire"). Defined dogmatically at Florence (1439)
and Trent (1563).

**Purgatory is condition, not place** in formal Catholic theology;
this lens treats it as a transitional plane for gameplay purposes,
following the wiki's Catholic worked example.

## PF2e plane mapping

A unique transitional plane, with no clean vanilla equivalent. The
closest analogue is a soul-cleansing version of the Boneyard, but
distinct from [[catholic:cosmology-sheol]] — Sheol was the pre-Christ
abode of the dead awaiting judgment; purgatory is the post-Christ
purification of the saved on their way to [[catholic:cosmology-heaven]].

The denizens are **the Church Suffering** (CCC §954–959), in
intercessory communion with the Church Triumphant (in heaven) and the
Church Militant (on earth). Living petitioners may offer prayers,
Masses, and indulgences for souls in purgatory — the practice
anchored in 2 Maccabees and refined through patristic and medieval
tradition.

## Campaign hooks

- A purgatorial dungeon-crawl is one of the cleanest level-15+
  capstones in the genre — souls progress through ascending
  purifications, each "level" a virtue being perfected. The PCs may
  be living intercessors, deceased on a guided ascent, or angels
  shepherding souls through.
- *Soothe* and *Restoration* function with extraordinary efficacy in
  purgatory — the place is, by nature, restorative.
- **The All Souls Day mechanic**: 2 November and the octave following
  are the canonical festival dates for purgatorial intercession.
  Indulgences for the dead are real-world Catholic practice; in
  fiction they translate cleanly to a campaign mechanic where
  parties can spend down-time gold or downtime hours to remit time
  for souls they are bound to.

## Honest theological note

Purgatory is rejected by Reformed and most Evangelical traditions
(Article XXII of the Anglican 39 Articles calls it "a fond thing,
vainly invented"). The Orthodox affirm an intermediate purification
without using the word "purgatory" and reject Catholic specifics
about the *poena*-vs-*culpa* distinction. **A campaign that uses
this plane has committed to a Catholic lens** — switching lenses
without retconning purgatory will break internal consistency.
