# Sheol / Hades — the abode of the dead, harrowed by Christ

The morally-neutral OT abode of the dead, rendered Hades by the LXX
and the New Testament; in Luke 16 it has compartments — a place of
comfort ("Abraham's bosom") and a place of torment, separated by a
great chasm. The Apostles' Creed confesses that Christ "descended
into hell" (*descendit ad inferos*) — the Catholic Catechism §631–637
clarifies that this means the *limbus patrum* of the just who awaited
the Redeemer. Christ harrowed it on Holy Saturday and led the
righteous departed into [[catholic:cosmology-heaven]].

## PF2e plane mapping

Maps to the **Boneyard** ferry zone — a now-largely-emptied
intermediate state for those who die before their particular judgment
is rendered. **Distinct from final Hell**
([[catholic:cosmology-gehenna]]); distinct from
[[catholic:cosmology-purgatory]] (which is the Catholic-distinctive
state of purification for the saved); distinct from the
[[catholic:cosmology-abyss]] which is reserved for fallen angels and
chaos-spirits.

In Catholic theology, the *limbo of the fathers* (limbus patrum) is
historically resolved — Christ emptied it. The disputed *limbo of
infants* (limbus infantium) is now treated by the Church as a
historical theological hypothesis without dogmatic standing
(International Theological Commission 2007).

## Campaign hooks

- A Cleric who descends to Sheol to harrow it walks the road of
  Christ between Friday and Sunday.
- *Raise Dead* and *Resurrect* rituals reach into Sheol or briefly
  into [[catholic:cosmology-purgatory]] — never into Heaven proper.
  Every resurrection is a soul recalled from intermediate rest, with
  the moral weight that implies.
