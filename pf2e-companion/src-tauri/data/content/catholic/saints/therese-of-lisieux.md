# Saint Thérèse of Lisieux — the Little Flower

Thérèse Martin (1873–1897), Carmelite nun of Lisieux, dead of
tuberculosis at twenty-four, declared Doctor of the Church by John
Paul II in 1997. Her *Little Way* — sanctity through small acts of
love offered with great trust — has made her one of the most
influential saints of the modern era. Patron of missions (though she
never left her cloister), of florists, and of those in spiritual
darkness. Her promise: "I will spend my heaven doing good upon
earth."

## Cleric / Oracle patron

Cleric chooses **Family** (her parents are also canonized) or
**Healing**. Oracle takes the **Life** mystery, with the Curse
flavored as her tubercular suffering and "night of nothingness."

- **Edicts (additional)**: do small things with great love, offer
  hidden sufferings as prayer for missions, trust in mercy without
  measure.
- **Anathema (additional)**: despise the small or hidden, demand
  spectacular signs of grace, despair in the dark night.

## Mechanical hooks

- *Soothe* and *Restoration* fit Thérèse's intercessory mode.
- A **shower of roses** narrative beat is the canonical Theresian
  miracle — flavor any granted petition this way.
- The cloistered Cleric (Doctrine: Cloistered) is her direct
  mechanical home.
