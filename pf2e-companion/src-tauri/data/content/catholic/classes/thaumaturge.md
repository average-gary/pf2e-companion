# Thaumaturge — the pilgrim of relics

The Thaumaturge's nine implements map **1:1 to Catholic relic
categories** that already exist as a fully-developed liturgical and
devotional tradition. **Zero rules change.** This is the elegant win
of the reskin set, and it is *more* on-the-nose for the Catholic lens
than for any other denominational lens — Catholic theology has the
formal *first-class / second-class / third-class* relic taxonomy,
canonized at Trent (1563) and elaborated by Aquinas (*ST* III q.25
a.6).

## Catholic relic taxonomy (in-fiction)

- **First-class relics**: the body of a saint, or a fragment thereof.
  **Highest mechanical bonus** in-fiction; rarest implement type.
- **Second-class relics**: items the saint owned, used, or wore in
  life (e.g. the cassock of [[catholic:therese-of-lisieux]]).
- **Third-class relics**: items that have **touched** a first- or
  second-class relic. The most accessible class — pilgrims acquire
  these from shrines.
- **Sacramentals**: Miraculous Medals, holy water, blessed salt,
  rosaries — not technically relics but the broader category of
  blessed objects with implement-flavor potential.

## Honest gotchas

- **The Thaumaturge is a pilgrim, not a sorcerer.** The class fantasy
  is exactly right: a saint who wields sacred objects, drawing on
  their consecrated power without claiming it as her own. This is the
  inverse of Simon Magus's transactional purchase (Acts 8). Frame the
  implement as something **given** — found on pilgrimage, inherited
  from a predecessor, granted by a vision, or dispensed by a bishop's
  authority.
- **No `pharmakeia` problem here.** The Thaumaturge's effects are
  sign-bearing, not drug-mediated; the implement is a token of faith,
  not a technique. Aquinas's q.96 a.4 (amulet rules — canonical
  Scripture only, no characters except the Cross) is the relevant
  patristic guidance for flavor; we don't enforce it mechanically.
- **Relic-trafficking** is the in-fiction crime that maps to abuse of
  Thaumaturge implements (cf. simony) — a great campaign-villain
  hook.
