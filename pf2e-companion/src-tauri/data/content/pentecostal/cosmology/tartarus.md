# Tartarus — the prison of the bound spirits

The deepest pit named in 2 Peter 2:4: the place where rebellious angels
are "delivered into chains of darkness, to be reserved unto judgment."
Jude 6 pairs with this verse. **Distinct from
[[pentecostal:cosmology-gehenna]]** (the eschatological lake of fire)
and **distinct from the wider [[pentecostal:cosmology-abyss]]** (the
present-age operational theatre).

## Canon note

The Pentecostal lens uses the **66-book canon**. The 1 Enoch tradition
that names the bound angels (Azazel in Dudael, Semjaza under the hills
for seventy generations, 1 Enoch 10) is **non-canonical** — but Jude
14-15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9 directly, and 2 Pet 2:4 / Jude 6 plainly
reference *some* angelic-binding tradition. Pentecostal practice varies:
many tables treat the Enochic detail as **edifying-but-extracanonical
backdrop** rather than doctrine. Use it for setting flavor without
quoting it as scripture.

## PF2e plane mapping

Maps to the **Tartarus floor of the Abyss** in PF2e nomenclature — a
sealed sub-layer holding the originally-fallen powers. The bound
remaindering means demonology in the Pentecostal lens is **two-tiered**:

- **Bound** (Tartarus) — the originally-fallen, presently-chained, kept
  for judgment day. Cannot directly act in the present age except in
  apocalyptic windows (Rev 9:1-11's locust release).
- **Loose** (Abyss) — the operational principalities and powers waging
  current war ([[pentecostal:cosmology-abyss]]). These are what
  deliverance ministry and *banishment* address.

## Campaign hooks

- A high-tier dungeon descent to a *currently sealed* Tartarus chamber
  is the campaign-scale equivalent of *opening* something that should
  stay closed — the Rev 9 key-of-the-bottomless-pit trope.
