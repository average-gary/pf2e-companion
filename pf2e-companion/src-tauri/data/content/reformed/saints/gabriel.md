# Gabriel the Archangel — exemplary witness

The second named archangel of the 66-book canon. Gabriel interprets
Daniel's visions and announces the conceptions of John the Baptist and
of Christ. Across the canonical record his role is the **herald who
delivers the Word** — the meaning-bearing messenger of providence at
covenantal hinge-points (Mosaic to Davidic in Daniel; Old to New in
Luke 1). This makes him a particularly resonant figure for a Reformed
setting precisely because his function *is* the proclamation of the
Word, which is the Reformed canonical instrument.

## Champion-cause distinction (read this)

As with [[reformed:michael]], Gabriel is **not a patron** in the
veneration sense. A Reformed Cleric or Oracle may take **Truth** or
**Knowledge** as their virtue-domain under YHWH, and may flavor their
calling around Gabriel-style proclamation, but the dedication is to
YHWH directly — the angel is a creature, not a co-deity (Calvin,
*Institutes* I.14.12). **No Raphael, no Uriel** in this canon: Tobit
and 2 Esdras / 1 Enoch are excluded from the Protestant 66 books.
