# Paul the Apostle — covenant herald, exemplary witness

Apostle to the Gentiles; the pen behind the Reformed reading of
salvation. Reformed theology is shaped by Paul more than by any other
biblical writer outside the Gospels — Romans is the Westminster /
Heidelberg / Three Forms of Unity backbone (justification by faith
alone, total depravity, election, the perseverance of the saints).
Paul is also the **canonical example of cessation in operation**: his
miracles (Acts 14:9-10, 19:11-12, 28:8) authenticated the apostolic
office and the closing of the canon — and the Reformed tradition
reads them as **non-repeatable apostolic signs**, not the ordinary
furniture of the post-canonical church (see the cessationism caveat
under [[reformed:yhwh]] and [[reformed:class-cleric]]).

## Champion-cause distinction (read this)

Paul exemplifies **Truth** (the gospel preached without compromise) and
**Faithfulness** (the long obedience of the missionary journeys), but
the Champion does not invoke him. His significance for the lens is
**doctrinal**: he is the apostolic witness whose closed canon is the
reason routine miracle-working is no longer the church's pattern.
