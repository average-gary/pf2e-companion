# Moses — covenant mediator, exemplary witness

Mediator of the Mosaic covenant ([[reformed:covenants]]); the prophet
who saw God's back (Ex 33:23); the lawgiver to whom the Decalogue was
given. Reformed theology reads Moses through the **threefold use of
the law** (Calvin, *Institutes* II.7.6-12): the law as mirror that
exposes sin, as restraint on civil evil, and as guide for the
sanctified life. Moses's **staff** — turned to a serpent, raised over
the Red Sea, struck against the rock — is the canonical image of
**signs done by petition**, not by technique (cf. the magic-theology
distinction in [[reformed:class-thaumaturge]]).

## Champion-cause distinction (read this)

Moses exemplifies **Justice** under YHWH (the giving of the law) and
faithful intercession (Ex 32:32, "blot me out of your book"), but is
not a Champion patron in this lens. His staff and tablets are
**covenant-tokens** — load-bearing flavor for [[reformed:class-thaumaturge]]
implements — but they are not relics in the venerated-object sense.
