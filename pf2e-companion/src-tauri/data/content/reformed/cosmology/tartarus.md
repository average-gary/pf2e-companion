# Tartarus — the prison of the fallen angels

Mentioned **once** in the Protestant canon, in 2 Peter 2:4: the deepest
pit, where rebellious angels are "kept in chains under darkness for
the judgment of the great day." Jude 6 is the parallel reference. The
Reformed lens stops there — the **1 Enoch Watcher mythology** (Azazel
under jagged rocks, Semjaza under the hills for seventy generations,
the named teaching of forbidden arts) is **non-canonical to the 66
books** and should be **flagged as in-world apocrypha** if used at all,
not naturalized as Author Truth (cf. the broader pack treatment in
[[reformed:cosmology-gehenna]] of grimoire-derived lore).

## PF2e plane mapping

Maps to a **sealed sub-layer of Hell** in this lens (rather than to a
distinct Tartarus floor of the Abyss as in fuller-mythology packs).
Reformed cosmology is **deliberately sparser** than Catholic / Orthodox
/ Lewisian: angel-or-demon, two destinations, one judgment. Tartarus
is the present-age binding of unspecified pre-flood rebels named only
as "angels who sinned" (2 Pet 2:4) — **deliberately not catalogued**.

## Reformed reasoning (read this)

Calvin (*Institutes* I.14.16) on demonology: "Whatever the Scripture
declares to us we must rest content with, lest, by inquiring further
than is permitted, we be found among those whom the Spirit has led
astray." The catalogue-of-bound-angels temptation — naming Azazel,
Semjaza, Armaros, etc. as encounter targets — is the precise kind of
speculative-demonology the Reformed tradition rejects. **Use Tartarus
sparingly**; treat 1 Enoch as a forbidden book that PCs *encounter* in
a wizard's library, not as the world's actual backstory.
