# Gehenna — the lake of fire

The Valley of Hinnom outside Jerusalem; the New Testament's name for
the eschatological place of punishment. The lake of fire of Revelation
20-21. Reformed eschatology holds the doctrine of **eternal conscious
punishment** (Westminster Confession XXXIII.2: "the wicked, who know
not God, and obey not the Gospel of Jesus Christ, shall be cast into
eternal torments"); annihilationism is a minority Reformed view, not
the confessional mainstream.

Read this against the Reformed doctrine of **double predestination**
(Westminster III; Calvin *Institutes* III.21-24): God has determined
some to glory and some to wrath, "to the praise of his glorious
justice." This is theologically uncomfortable in modernity, and a GM
should know which side of it the table sits on before campaigning
heavily here.

## PF2e plane mapping

Maps to **Hell** (the lawful evil layer in vanilla PF2e). The
"lawful" framing is appropriate to Reformed theology — Gehenna is the
**verdict of a holy and just God rendered against unrepentant sin**,
not a chaos-domain. Reformed angelology is suspicious of elaborate
demon-court taxonomies (Goetia, *Lesser Key of Solomon* — see the
sister wiki note on grimoires); use sparing canon-attested names
(Beelzebub from Mark 3:22, Satan / the dragon / the accuser; **not**
Asmodeus from Tobit, **not** Belial from non-canonical lists). If
your table wants the richer infernal roster, that is a Catholic-lens
preference and the sister pack covers it.

## Mechanical notes

- *Banishment* sends infernal creatures here as their original plane.
- The Reformed lens is **strongly disinclined to depict in-character
  demonic dialogue** — Calvin treated demonic conversation as the
  paradigmatic forbidden engagement (cf. Saul at Endor, 1 Sam 28). NPC
  devils in this lens *attack*; they do not bargain.
